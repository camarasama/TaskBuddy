Section 1Why TaskBuddy exists

Almost every household with school-age children runs the same failed experiment. Someone draws up a chore chart. It works for about two weeks. Then it stops being updated, nobody can remember who did what, and the whole thing collapses back into asking three times and doing it yourself.

The chart is not the problem. The problem is that a chart records intentions and nothing else. It cannot tell you whether the bin actually went out, it gives a child nothing back for the effort, and it turns every disagreement into one person's word against another's.

The five things that actually go wrong

Nobody can prove what happened, so every week ends in an argument.

Every task is submitted by the child and approved or returned by a parent. The record is kept, so "I did do it" stops being a matter of memory.

Effort is invisible. A child tidies up for a month and has nothing to show for it.

Approved work pays experience points that fill a level bar and never go away, plus spendable points. Progress is visible on the child's home screen the moment a parent approves.

Pocket money is disconnected from contribution, so it stops meaning anything.

Parents set the rewards and what they cost. Children choose what to save towards, and see exactly how far off they are.

Motivation collapses the first time a child misses a day.

Streaks come with a grace period, and a long streak earns a freeze that absorbs one missed day automatically. One bad evening does not undo three weeks.

The apps that exist are built for five-year-olds or for office workers.

TaskBuddy is built for 10 to 16 specifically. No sticker charts, no cartoon animals, and no project-management vocabulary either.

What we deliberately did not build

A rewards app can very easily turn into a slot machine. Two decisions push against that, and they are the ones we most want tested.

  • Games cannot out-earn chores. The quiz games pay real points, but spendable points are capped at once per subject per day, and the amounts are set so that a full day of nothing but quizzes earns less than a normal day of chores. Playing instead of helping is not a winning strategy, by design.
  • There is no way to spend real money anywhere in the app. Not on points, not on avatar items, not on anything. Every reward comes from a parent, and every cosmetic comes from points that were earned.
The one-sentence version

TaskBuddy turns household chores into something a child can see themselves getting better at, and gives the parent a record instead of an argument.

Section 2If you are one of our testers

Thank you. TaskBuddy is in a closed test on Google Play, which means the app is not publicly listed and is visible only to the Google accounts we have added to the tester list.

Getting the app

  1. Open the link from the invitation email on your Android phone.
  2. Make sure the phone is signed in to Google with the exact address the invitation was sent to. This is the single most common thing that goes wrong. On any other Google account the Play page will say the app is not available, which means the account is wrong, not that anything is broken.
  3. Tap Install. There is no separate step to accept or confirm.
What we most need from you

Open the app on a few different days rather than exploring it thoroughly once. Google counts days with activity, and a habit app can only really be judged over a week. If something is confusing, that is a finding worth reporting, not a mistake on your part.

Reporting a problem

Whatever you can give us is useful. What helps most:

  • What you were trying to do, and what happened instead.
  • Whether you were the parent or the child at the time.
  • Roughly when it happened, so we can find it in the logs.
  • A screenshot, if the screen looked wrong.

Please read section 8 first. One thing is known to be missing in this build and is already on the list, so there is no need to report it.

Section 3How TaskBuddy works

The loop

A parent creates a task and assigns it to a child. The child does it in real life and marks it complete in the app. The parent gets a notification, opens Approvals, and either approves it or sends it back with a note. On approval the child immediately receives their XP and points, along with any level-up, badge, streak or team bonus that the approval triggered.

That cycle is the whole product. Everything else supports it.

Two currencies, on purpose

CurrencyEarnedSpentPurpose
XP On approval, and from games Never Fills the level bar. Permanent record of everything a child has done.
Points On approval, plus level-up, streak, game and teamwork bonuses In the reward shop and on avatar items The spendable currency.

Keeping them separate means a child saving up for something expensive never has to choose between levelling up and buying it. Spending points does not cost them progress.

Who can do what

RoleSigns in withCan
Parent Email and password Everything: tasks, rewards, approvals, children, settings, reports
Co‑parent Email and password The same as a parent. Only the original account holder can remove a co-parent.
Child Family code, username and a 4-digit PIN Their own tasks, rewards, games and profile. No email address needed.

Android app or website

Both work, on the same account, at the same time. The Android app is what you are testing and it covers the whole loop, including photos. The website adds a couple of things the phone does not have, listed in section 8.

Section 4Parent guideParent

4.1 Creating your account

  1. Open the app and choose I'm a parent, then Create an account.
  2. Enter your name, email, date of birth, family name and a password. Passwords must be at least 8 characters, and we refuse any password known to have appeared in a public breach.
  3. Verify your email using the link we send you.
  4. A family code is generated for your household, in the style MEGA-VIPER-8481. Your children use it to sign in. You can see it any time under Children.

4.2 Confirming you are the parent

Before you can add a child, we need a verified record that a parent agreed to it. This is a legal requirement for holding data about children, and it is a one-off step for the whole family.

  1. Fill in the add-child form as normal and submit it.
  2. The app will tell you that confirmation is needed, and send you the email automatically at that moment.
  3. Tap Confirm I'm the parent to see the status screen, where you can also send a fresh email if the first went astray.
  4. Follow the link in the email. Come back to the app and add your child.
Note

The confirmation link is single-use and expires. If yours has timed out, just try adding a child again and a new email is sent.

4.3 The setup guide

A four-step guide walks you through adding a child, creating a task and approving it. The last step is worth doing rather than skipping: it seeds a task the child has already "finished" and hands you the approve button, so you see the full loop, with real points, in the first minute. Your progress is saved on the server, so you can close the app and a co-parent sees the same checklist.

Settings, Calendar, Insights, Reports, Co-parents and the consent screen are all reached from within those tabs rather than sitting on the bar, because six tabs do not fit comfortably on a phone.

4.5 Adding a child

From Children, tap Add a child. You will need:

  • First and last name.
  • A username, 3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers and underscore only. This is what they type to sign in, so two siblings both called Sam need different ones.
  • Date of birth. TaskBuddy is for ages 10 to 16 and this is enforced.
  • A 4-digit PIN. When editing later, leaving the PIN blank keeps the existing one rather than clearing it.

No email address is needed for a child, and they never receive email from us.

4.6 Getting your child signed in

From Children, open the family code screen. It shows a QR code and the code as text underneath.

  • On a phone, your child taps Scan my parent's code and points their camera at it.
  • If the camera is unavailable or refused, they can type the code instead. The QR is a shortcut, never the only way in.
  • For the website, read the code out. Web child login has no QR.

The same screen lists signed-in devices for you and your children, and lets you sign a device out. Co-parents cannot be signed out from here, deliberately.

4.7 Creating tasks

  • Give it a title, description and a points value. Difficulty follows from the points automatically.
  • Choose primary (must-do) or bonus. Bonus tasks stay locked for a child until their primary tasks for the day are done.
  • Optionally set a start time, an estimated duration and a due date.
  • Assign it to one or more children.
  • Browse ideas opens a library of ready-made chores grouped by age and room. Picking one fills the form and you can change anything before saving.

Team-up tasks

Assign two or more children to one job. Each child still earns the full points for their own work, and the teamwork bonus is paid on top, to everyone, once every member has been approved. Nobody earns less for co-operating and nobody is rewarded for going last.

Limits that are deliberate

  • A child can hold at most 3 active assignments, and only 1 primary at a time. This is a focus guard, not a technical limit.
  • If a new task overlaps something already scheduled for that child, you are shown the clash and can adjust or proceed anyway.
  • Tasks can repeat daily, weekly, on weekdays or at weekends. New instances are generated automatically each night.

4.8 Approving work

You get a notification as soon as a child submits. Open Approvals, review what they sent, then approve or return it with a written note. Approving pays out everything at once: points, XP, level-up, badges, streak and any team bonus. Returning it puts the task back with your feedback so they can fix it and resubmit.

Note

If a co-parent has already handled a submission, the screen tells you who dealt with it rather than showing an error.

4.9 Rewards

  • Set what each reward costs in points, how many times each child may claim it, and optionally a household-wide limit and an expiry date.
  • Browse ideas offers ready-made suggestions ordered by what families actually redeem.
  • A collaborative reward is funded by several children pooling points towards one goal. When you create it you choose who receives it: shared with the family, or one child you nominate once it is funded.
  • When a child redeems something you are notified. Mark it fulfilled once you have actually delivered it.

4.10 Calendar, Insights and Reports

  • Calendar shows the week with your children as columns, and flags two tasks that overlap for the same child.
  • Insights answers what a table cannot: which days actually work for your family, which tasks are quietly never completed, and whether the points you award have drifted out of step with what your rewards cost.
  • Reports covers task completion, the points ledger, redemptions, engagement and streaks, achievements, the leaderboard, overdue tasks, execution time and games.
Reports work differently on the phone

In the app, Reports is a download screen: you choose the filters and it hands you a CSV or PDF to open or share. There is no on-screen chart or table. That is a deliberate decision rather than something missing. Use the website if you want to read a report on screen.

From a child's page you can also download a one-page monthly report card: their streaks, badges, growth against the previous month and a note from you. It is meant to be shared with a co-parent or a grandparent.

4.11 Notifications, quiet hours and schooltime

Settings controls which emails you receive. Separately, each child has a quiet hours window overnight and an optional schooltime window on chosen weekdays, during which their device will not buzz.

Set your time zone first

Quiet hours are read in your family time zone, so set that in Settings before relying on them. Notifications still arrive in the app during a quiet window, they simply do not interrupt, and nothing is delivered late in a burst afterwards.

TaskBuddy also limits itself: beyond one lifecycle email a day and three a week, further non-essential emails are held back. Account emails such as verification, password reset, consent, invitations and security notices are never held back, and neither is your Monday summary.

4.12 Settings and co-parents

  • Family code — view or regenerate. Regenerating stops the old code working immediately.
  • Time zone — used by quiet hours and schooltime.
  • Streak grace period — extra hours past midnight that still count towards the previous day.
  • Leaderboard — turn sibling rankings on or off for the whole family.
  • Daily game points cap — the most a child can earn from games in one day.
  • Two-factor authentication — add a one-time code from an authenticator app to your sign-in.
  • Invite a co-parent — they receive a secure link valid for 7 days, and once accepted have equal access.
  • Webhooks and referrals — send TaskBuddy events to another service, and share a referral link. Referrals earn a badge, never points.

Section 5Child guideChild

This part is written for you. TaskBuddy is where you pick up jobs, get them signed off, level up and spend what you earn.

5.1 Signing in

  1. Open the app and choose I'm a kid.
  2. Tap Scan my parent's code and point the camera at the QR code your parent shows you. The app will ask for camera permission at that moment, not before.
  3. If there is no camera, or you would rather type, ask your parent to read the family code out.
  4. Enter your username and your 4-digit PIN.

Your session stays signed in, so you do not have to do this every time. If you forget your PIN, tap Forgot PIN and your parent will be told.

5.2 Your five tabs

HomeYour level and XP bar, points, streak, today's tasks and your goal

RewardsWhat you can spend points on
GamesQuizzes that earn extra points and XP

5.3 Doing a task

  1. Open Tasks and read what is being asked.
  2. Tap Start when you begin, so the time you spend is recorded.
  3. Do the job in real life.
  4. Tick it off. You can add a short note if you want to tell your parent something about it.
  5. If the task asks for a photo, take one or pick one from your gallery before you send it.
  6. It moves to waiting for approval until a parent reviews it.
Photos

Some tasks ask for a photo so your grown-up can see the job is done. Tap Take a photo or Choose a photo in the box that appears when you tick the task off. If your phone will not let the app use the camera, you can still finish the task without one.

Primary and bonus tasks

Primary tasks are the ones that must be done. Bonus tasks unlock once your primary tasks for the day are finished, and you claim them yourself from the available list.

Team-up tasks

Some tasks are shared with a brother or sister. You each get the full points for your own work, and everyone gets the teamwork bonus once all of you have been approved.

Lost signal?

You can still start and complete tasks. They are saved on your phone and sent automatically when you are back online, so nothing is lost on a patchy connection.

5.4 Rewards

  • Rewards you can afford are highlighted. The others show how many more points you need.
  • Tap the heart to add something to your wishlist, so your parents know what you actually want.
  • Set one as your goal and your progress towards it shows on your home screen.
  • Tap redeem to claim it. Your points are deducted and a parent is told to deliver it.

Some rewards are shared, so you and your siblings can each put points in until it is paid for. A reward can also show as sold out if the family limit has been reached, or expired if its date has passed.

5.5 Games

Six subjects — maths, science, geography, vocabulary, grammar and puzzles — each at three levels: beginner, intermediate and hard. You can pick any level at any age. The questions rotate, so coming back tomorrow gives you a fresh set.

  • You find out straight away whether an answer was right, and what the correct one was.
  • Answers lock once chosen, so take your time.
  • You need to get at least 3 out of 5 right for a game to pay anything at all. Below that, clicking at random earns nothing.
  • Above that, points scale with how many you got right, and XP is paid in full.
How much games actually pay

Beginner pays 2 points, intermediate 3, hard 4. Spendable points are paid once per subject per day. Play the same subject again and you still earn XP, just not more points. Your parents also set a daily cap on total game points.

Games are meant to top up what you earn from chores, not replace it. Review and history screens let you go back over questions you got wrong.

5.6 Me

  • Achievements — badges unlock automatically when you hit a milestone.
  • Avatar items — bought with points you earned. Wearing a new hat takes the old one off, but you still own it. Nothing here costs real money and nothing ever will.
  • Leaderboard — how you and your siblings compare. Your parents can switch this off.
  • My week — what you finished, your best day, your streak and new badges. Look for it at the weekend.
  • Notifications — everything the app has told you.

5.7 Streaks

Your streak counts days in a row where you finished at least one task. Reach 7, 14, 30 or 100 days and you get a celebration. Keep a long streak going and you earn a streak freeze, which covers one missed day automatically. Your longest streak is always kept, even if the current one breaks.

Section 7Privacy and safety

  • Verified parental consent is required before any child account can be created, and it is checked on the server every time, not just in the app.
  • Children have no email address in TaskBuddy and are never emailed.
  • Photos a child submits are private. They are stored so that only your family can retrieve them.
  • A child's chosen profile picture is held for parent approval before it becomes visible.
  • No real-money purchases exist anywhere in the app, and no advertising.
  • You can export everything. A parent can download a full JSON bundle of the family's data.
  • Sessions are visible and revocable. The devices screen lists every signed-in device for you and your children.

Parents are told plainly in the app that they can see when a child signs in and roughly what they do, because monitoring anyone without saying so is not something we are willing to do.

Section 8Known gaps in this build

One known gap, already on the list. No need to report it.

Avatar item artwork

Avatar items show as their names rather than pictures. Buying, owning and equipping all work correctly; the artwork simply has not shipped yet.

Two more differences worth knowing, which are decisions rather than gaps:

  • Reports in the app download a file rather than displaying on screen. See 4.10.
  • Children have no comment thread in the app. They can add a note when completing a task, and comment threads are on the website.
